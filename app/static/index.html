01 The Hidden Threat

What Is "Ghost Gear" and Why Is It Devastating?

Ghost gear refers to commercial fishing gear—nets, longlines, and crab pots—accidentally lost or abandoned at sea. Made from non-biodegradable synthetic polymers (nylon, polypropylene), these nets continue trapping and killing marine mammals, apex sharks, sea turtles, and corals for hundreds of years.

02 Subsea Sensing

How Does Side-Scan Sonar (SSS) See in Zero Visibility?

Light attenuates rapidly in ocean water, rendering optical cameras blind past 5 to 10 meters. Side-Scan Sonar emits dual-sided acoustic fan beams (port and starboard). Hard objects, synthetic ropes, and wrecks backscatter sound violently (bright highlights) while casting distinct acoustic shadows behind them.

03 Artificial Intelligence

Why Use a Multi-Stage Voting Architecture?

Single detection stages easily confuse natural geological ridges and boulders with man-made debris. Our pipeline deploys two specialized stages in concert: Stage A (Anomaly Detection) performs initial anomaly verification ("Is it artificial?"), while Stage B (Classification) identifies specific subsea classes (ghost net, crab pot, shipwreck, mine). Detections are IoU-clustered and evaluated against a rigorous voting decision matrix.

IoU Cluster Merging False Positive Elimination
Dual-Stage Consensus

20.5M combined parameters voting deterministically to prevent false alarms on natural seabed reefs.

04 Precision Salvage

How Are Recovery Crews Guided to the Target?

Underwater retrieval operations cannot waste hours searching blind. Using nadir altitude, boat heading, survey latitude/longitude, and pixel cross-track resolution, our geolocation system computes exact target GPS coordinates without ever hallucinating coordinates if survey telemetry is incomplete.
